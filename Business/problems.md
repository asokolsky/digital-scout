# Practical Business and Investment Problems

## Wash Sale

Q: A [wash sale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wash_sale) is a sale of securities
"at a loss when substantially identical securities were acquired within 30 days
before or after the sale." Why does the IRS disallow the loss?

A: To prevent you from claiming a tax loss while keeping the same investment.
The sale is legal; the loss is generally added to the new shares' basis and
postponed until those shares are sold.

## Tax Gain Harvesting

Q: [Tax gain harvesting](https://www.bogleheads.org/wiki/Tax_gain_harvesting) is
the opposite idea: sell appreciated stock, realize the gain, and buy it back.
Wash-sale rules do not apply to gains.

You are an individual in your 20s with little to no income.
You've got a gift of stocks from your parents, say ~$10K.
Why would you be interested in harvesting your gains?

A: You may be able to realize a long-term gain in the 0% federal bracket and
reset the stock's basis higher. [Gifted stock](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
generally keeps the donor's basis and holding period.

Caveat: state tax and the [kiddie tax](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8615)
may apply. A full-time student under 24 whose parents provide most of the support
may have investment income taxed at the parents' rate.

## Equivalent Rate of Return

Q: Delaying Social Security from age 67 to age 70 results in about a 24% benefit
increase. What is the equivalent yearly rate of return?
Ignore the years of forgone payments, and the longevity break-even analysis.

A: `(1.24)^(1/3) - 1 = 7.44%`. The benefit is also inflation-adjusted.
